The COVID-19 pandemic has intensified concerns about declining mental health, exacerbated by widespread social isolation. Due to limitations on social connections and adjustments to daily schedules, people are experiencing higher levels of stress, anxiety, and loneliness. The sudden change to online communication and remote work has further blurred the lines between work and personal life, increasing feelings of isolation and making mental health issues worse. 
This project aims to address prevalent mental health issues, particularly anxiety, within online interactions. Through the development of a Machine Learning model, the research seeks to recognize anxiety symptoms from various digital communication sources such as tweets, chats, comments, and short messages. By employing advanced machine learning techniques, it demonstrates the feasibility of accurately identifying anxiety in short text. Evaluation will assess the model's performance across diverse language contexts. A dataset comprising various emotions is used to study the different features of sentiments and achieve the objectives. ML algorithms tend to uncover hidden or concealed patterns, analyze these sentiments and present the output more understandably. This project contributes to understanding emotion recognition and technology-driven mental health interventions, particularly in the post-pandemic landscape. Application of supervised models such as Logistic regression, K-Nearest neighbour, Naïve Bayes, Decision tree and SVM is done to compare the accuracy of models and plotting confusion matrices to understand in depth how depression or anxiety would be detected from short text on social media platforms.  Furthermore, the project compares pre-trained Large Language Models (LLM) for detecting depression in text, expanding insights into Deep Learning/AI-driven mental health detection methods. Based on the findings, this comparative analysis helps to provide a comprehensive understanding of the strengths and limitations of different models in the detection of depression from text, informing the development of more strong and effective ways for mental health assessment and intervention in this current digital age.
